Why El Paso businesses miss calls

El Paso has a detail most Texas phone setups miss: the city is on Mountain Time, one hour behind Dallas and Houston. If your answering rules assume Central Time, your after-hours message can start an hour early. Add cross-border family ties, shift workers around Fort Bliss and busy clinics, and calls arrive well outside a normal 9-to-5.

Which El Paso businesses benefit most?

  • Medical and dental offices: Education and health care is the biggest employment group here. Practices can answer morning rushes and book in English or Spanish.
  • HVAC and contractors: Desert summers make a failed AC urgent. Calls can be flagged and sent to your on-call tech.
  • Law firms: Immigration, family and injury callers often need a first response in Spanish, at any hour.

What to check before you go live

  1. Test real calls: ask awkward questions, not only easy ones, and listen to how it handles them.
  2. Set the rules: decide which calls transfer to a person and which become messages.
  3. Plan your minutes: estimate your busiest month, not an average one.
  4. Tell callers what you need to: Texas is generally treated as a one-party consent state for recording calls, but rules differ for callers in other states, so check with your advisor if you record calls.
